# Workbench Template - Blueprint

## Product Destination

Workbench Template is a copyable reference installation of LLM Workbench: a starting structure for new projects and a real installed room for independently checking explicit harness updates. It explains its files' ownership.

## People And Problems Served

Project owners need a legible foundation; arriving agents need authority, boundaries and navigable owners; maintainers need installed behavior they can verify separately from the producer. Plain files serve all three.

## Promised Outcomes

Readers understand each control, lane and collection, find its owner, and distinguish reusable structure from local history. Copies can be deliberately adapted to named products. Explicit updates preserve identity, evidence and intentional differences while exposing compatibility and acceptance gaps.

## Desired Experience And Behavior

One local command explains the manifest-defined room. The optional tour orients; it adds no authority or required future application. Agents enter through AGENTS, Runbook, Architecture, Glossary and relevant Wiki, then TASKBOARD and its current owners. The generated board grants no authority. Scope stays explicit, including specification-only requests. Significant context, decisions and corrections become source-linked Wiki jots before dependent output; legacy JSON stays readable and recipient handoffs use Markdown.

## Integrated System Design

[System design and runtime constraints](workbench/docs/TEMPLATE_SYSTEM_DESIGN.md) explain root owners, managed skills, history, local operation and the optional tour.

## Cross-Cutting Qualities And Constraints

No application data, secrets or personal content belongs in a starter. It has tracked and ignored support state: schema examples, durable records and authorized shared jots are tracked; private jots, legacy local notes, handoffs and recovery stay ignored. Seeding, migration, backup and private synchronization require scoped authority.

Source/tests prove behavior; controls/assigned owners define intent. Managed updates retain receipts and recovery. Historical provenance differs from installed identity; files or structural checks alone prove neither native behavior, delivery nor agent reliability.

## Desired Lifecycle

Read/run locally. Updates compare exact reviewed upstream controls, preserve differences/evidence, exercise installed workflows and obtain independent review before integration; main remains owner-controlled. History stays accessible. Product adaptation needs its own intent/verification. An update proves that update, not automated personalization, Genesis or a fresh-project workflow.

## Non-Goals

No fictional Example application, portfolio manager, replacement for upstream tests or universal design tutorial. No required paid service, private catalog, Foundry or network for ordinary local work. No self-publication, unrelated-room updates, copied personal context or universal agent-compliance claim.
